The motion, drawn 腕

default axis
Shoulder follows the phone. Elbow and wrist follow late — that lag is the whip. Set both to 1 and the arm moves as one stick, which shows you what the lag is doing. Replay slowly to find where the hand is quickest. Which way the bones lie comes from the axis measured in Setup; until you lock one in, this assumes the arm runs along the phone's top edge.

Auto finds your shoulder from the acceleration itself, which is usually right. The other four force the arm axis to a fixed face of the phone — reach for those only if you gripped it oddly and the arc looks wrong.
Release is taken as the last big rotation peak — the wrist snap, which happens within a few milliseconds of the ball leaving. Speed and angle at that instant are all gravity needs for the rest. The angle is the shakiest input, since it depends on how the phone sat in your grip, so override it if the arc looks wrong. No spin, no drag — a bare gravity arc.

Measure your arm 較正

--m

distance from the joint you swing around

idle

how tidy --0–1
Arm straight, swing full circles from the shoulder — over the top and down through the bottom, like a windmill, not just side to side. The more of the circle you cover, the better the shoulder direction below comes out too. Force equals turn rate squared times radius, so the slope of those dots is how far the phone is from your shoulder — no drift, nothing added up. Tuck your elbow to your side and measure again and you should get roughly your forearm, which is a good check that it's working.

Where your arm is 腕軸

wobble --°
shoulder direction --
not set
The same circles that measure your arm also say which way your shoulder lies. Because the phone turns with your arm, the shoulder stays in one spot of the phone’s own frame — that is the green arrow, and the clay dots are the readings it was fitted to. Wobble under about five degrees means a clean circle. Locking it in gives throws a fallback for the slow part of the wind-up, where there is too little force to read the direction live. In a hurry, or the circle keeps coming out wobbly? Hold your arm straight out in your throwing grip, stay still, and tap Hold Straight instead — it reads the axis off that one pose directly.
